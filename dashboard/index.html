Coral condition and heat

Coral cover by survey year

Mean live coral cover of the units surveyed each year. The 39 units surveyed in both 2024 and 2025 changed by −4.76 pp on average; the 2025 mean is 39.8%.

Limit: Different units are surveyed each year (only 2 in 2012), so this is not a fixed national panel.
Source: Reef Check Malaysia annual survey reports, 2012–2025.

Regional heat stress (NOAA)

Mean regional maximum Degree Heating Weeks (DHW) in each survey year. Heat passed the bleaching alert level (DHW 4) in 2016, 2019, 2020, 2023 and 2024, peaking at 11.8 in 2024.

Limit: Regional satellite context, not in-water readings at each island; not used in the model.
Source: NOAA Coral Reef Watch 5 km regional virtual stations.

Tourism and reef value

Marine-park visitors and the data gap

Recorded domestic and foreign visitors to marine parks in Johor, Kedah, Labuan, Pahang and Terengganu, rising from 423,000 in 2000 to a peak of 863,000 in 2016 (737,000 in 2017). No official series continues after 2017; island arrivals are published for only 11 units in 2024.

Limit: State totals; they cannot be assigned to individual reefs or linked to reef change over time.
Source: Department of Marine Park Malaysia via data.gov.my (five state datasets).

Published value of marine-park reefs

Seven annual value components for six marine-park archipelagos, published at RM8.7 billion a year. The value placed on reef scenery (aesthetic, RM8.0 billion) far exceeds recorded tourism income (RM4.6 million): reef tourism has wide potential still to be realised and preserved.

Limit: 2011–2015 study, largely willingness-to-pay; not current revenue or visitor spending.
Source: Department of Marine Park Malaysia, Total Economic Value of Marine Biodiversity (pp. 6–13).

Factors linked to coral change

Heat and reported impacts vs next change

Higher heat has a weak link with the next coral change (Spearman ρ −0.10, n = 348, p = 0.051). Reports of anchor, trash or bleaching damage shift the next change by less than 1 pp/yr.

Limit: Descriptive associations, not causal effects.
Source: Reef Check Malaysia; NOAA Coral Reef Watch.

All measured factors vs next change

Spearman correlation of the 22 model inputs and 2 NOAA variables with the next coral-cover change. The strongest are cover versus regional average (ρ −0.32), live coral cover (−0.30) and last change rate (−0.22); most others are weak.

Limit: Unadjusted single-factor links; not a ranking of causes.
Source: Reef Check Malaysia; NOAA Coral Reef Watch.

What the model relies on

How much forward-test error rises when each input is shuffled. Cover versus regional average and the last change rate matter most.

Limit: Predictive importance, not cause.
Source: ReefSafe model, past-only validation 2021–2025.

Model check

Model comparison

Forward-test error of four models on the same past-only folds. Gradient boosting has the lowest MAE: 5.862 vs 5.991 pp/yr for the mean baseline, a 2.1% gain.

Limit: A small gain: use it to rank field checks, not as a forecast.
Source: ReefSafe model, past-only validation 2021–2025.

Predicted vs observed change

Forward-test predictions against the change later observed (183 cases). Predictions stay near the centre and miss large gains and losses.

Limit: Weak screening signal; not a precise forecast.
Source: ReefSafe model, past-only validation 2021–2025.
